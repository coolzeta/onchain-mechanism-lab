## Which price is the arbitrageur repairing?

When USD-LAB trades below $1 while the protocol still permits redemption near $1, an arbitrageur may:

1. buy USD-LAB cheaply in the market;
2. redeem it for reserve assets under the protocol rule;
3. keep the difference while shrinking supply.

This path does not guarantee that price returns to $1 immediately. It depends on available reserves, redemption not being paused, market liquidity, and clean price inputs.

<Concept title="Supply contraction is pressure, not magic">
<p>Redemption burns tokens and reduces tradable supply, but supply reduction cannot create reserves from nothing. If reserves are untrusted or exits are restricted, an arbitrageur will not take risk just because the target is written as 1.00.</p>
</Concept>

<MechanismDetails>

```text
marketPrice < targetPrice
  → buy discounted stablecoin
  → redeem under the protocol rule
  → burn supply and receive reserve
```

Arbitrage turns a price gap into a behavioral incentive, but it cannot replace reserve proof, liquidity, or correct outside data. Check “can arbitrage” and “can redeem” as separate conditions.

</MechanismDetails>
